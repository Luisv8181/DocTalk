# DocTalk Vision

## The problem
Medical mental-health resources are abundant but fragmented. A student, resident, or physician may have to search a university site, hospital intranet, professional organization, nonprofit, state program, research database, and peer community just to understand what help exists.

## The vision
DocTalk becomes an open, living map of the mental-health support ecosystem around medicine.

A person should be able to arrive with a real-world need and quickly answer:

**What exists? Is it for someone like me? Can I access it? Can I trust the source? What kind of evidence supports it? What are its limitations?**

## Who it serves
Primary:
- medical students
- resident physicians
- practicing physicians

Secondary:
- fellows and early-career clinicians
- educators and GME/UME leaders
- counselors, psychologists, social workers, and other mental-health professionals
- researchers
- institutions

## What makes DocTalk different
DocTalk does not try to become every resource. It organizes the ecosystem around the person seeking help.

Its differentiators are:
- provenance
- transparent evidence classification
- audience-aware discovery
- geography and access metadata
- privacy-aware presentation
- open contribution
- machine-readable knowledge
- future intelligent retrieval constrained by the curated catalog

## Long-term possibility
The repository can become a public knowledge substrate for:
- a searchable resource website
- specialty-specific collections
- curriculum builders
- institutional resource directories
- research discovery
- educational tools
- an AI-assisted navigator that cites the underlying resources

The AI layer should never become the authority. The curated sources remain the authority.

## Design philosophy
**Find the right door, faster.**

The project should reduce the cognitive and logistical burden of finding help without reducing complex mental-health decisions to simplistic scores or automated diagnoses.
